About Me

Hello, my name is Aadrika Sharma, and I am from Cary, NC. I’m a freshman at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, majoring in Community and Global Public Health. I have always been deeply involved in service to my community. When I was younger, I started off with service through volunteering at food banks and local swim clubs in the Raleigh area, but my service initiatives became more specific to community health as I got older. In high school, I got deeply involved in anti-vaping initiatives through the POE Center of Health Education after witnessing students having health complications such as vaping-related lipoid pneumonia, and observing the minimal education provided about the health risks of vaping specifically in schools throughout Wake County. Through the POE Center of Health Education, I advocated against teen vaping by speaking about the dangers of vaping in the Gen-V-Teen Vaping in North Carolina documentary and presenting at the Wake County Tobacco Free Community Forum about the link between vaping and lipoid pneumonia in children under 18. I also led an initiative at my school called Escape the Vape, where I led clubs to host anti-vaping-related events and organized guest speakers throughout the week. The work on anti-vaping initiatives also led me to create my own Water Safety organization, where I volunteered to present about how to stay safe on beaches and pools. Through my volunteering and advocacy work, I learned skills in organizing community events and communication in public speaking. Acquiring these skills made me more driven and passionate about continuing to advocate for healthy habits.

Through my major in college, I hope to continue serving the communities around me through education and advocacy efforts. With my major, I will be able to learn more about how to design programs in communities for health education and identify specific communities in which specific health disparities take place. I am interested in furthering my knowledge in designing health education programs so I can continue working towards lowering teen vaping in local communities and expand on the work that I have already done within communities in high school. I am also deeply interested in connecting personal medicine with public health to then see the connections between individual health problems and the trends that are seen within the whole community. I have recently joined the Schisler lab, which studies STUB1/CHIP mutations in SCA48. Working with the scientific and medical side of research will give me a closer look at the individual side of health, which I can pair with the community-wide view I gain in public health.

For fun, I was deeply involved with my high school band, playing the clarinet. I was part of the wind ensemble and marching band in high school. I was also a part of the student ambassador program at my school, where I helped organize the Shamrock Shindig event for new students to network with each other. Some of my hobbies include baking, trying new cafes, traveling, and working out/taking long walks!
